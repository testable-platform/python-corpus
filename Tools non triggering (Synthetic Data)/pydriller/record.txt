Record of burning at the lime
=============================

The draws are counted at the close of a turn, never at the start of the
next one. Counted late, the figure drifts with whatever happened overnight
at the lime.

When the draws were reported to one decimal place the instrument resolved
only to whole units. The decimal carried nothing, and someone at the lime
eventually explained it anyway.

A lot rejected at burning is held, not returned the same day. The lime
learned that a same-day return loses the evidence of why the draws were
rejected at burning in the first place.

The draws are the unit of account because they can be counted without
being moved. Everything else the lime tracks is derived from the draws,
and each derivation is written down.

The oldest holding of draws sets the review date, not the largest. Age is
what turns a holding into a problem; volume decides only how large the
draws problem will be.

Work not finished by the end of the opening hour at the lime is raised,
not carried quietly on. Carried quietly it surfaces at burning, where it
costs a turn instead of an hour.

Kept as a plain record of the draws. Written as a program it would give
the roster's parsers something to read at the lime.
