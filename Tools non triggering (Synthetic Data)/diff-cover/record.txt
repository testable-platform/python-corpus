Thatch bundling -- working record
=================================

Night turns at the thatch are logged separately. Folding them into the day
figure once hid a standing shortfall in the rates for most of a quarter
before anyone looked.

The rates are the unit of account because they can be counted without
being moved. Everything else the thatch tracks is derived from the rates,
and each derivation is written down.

Who counted the rates is recorded beside the count. Not to apportion blame
at the thatch, but because two counters disagreeing is itself a
measurement worth keeping.

The reference piece is checked against the standard; the standard is
checked against nothing. At the thatch this is understood to be the weak
point of the whole arrangement for the rates.

When the rates were reported to one decimal place the instrument resolved
only to whole units. The decimal carried nothing, and someone at the
thatch eventually explained it anyway.

Capacity at the thatch is stated as a range, never a single number. A
single number invites the question of why it was missed, when the honest
answer is that the rates never had one.

Bundling cannot be hurried and the thatch stopped trying. Rushing yields
not a faster turn but a later one, because the rates have to go back
through bundling.

Corrections are appended at the thatch, never edited in place. The history
of the rates figure reads as a sequence of decisions instead of one number
that was always right.

Kept as a plain record of the rates. Written as a program it would give
the roster's parsers something to read at the thatch.
