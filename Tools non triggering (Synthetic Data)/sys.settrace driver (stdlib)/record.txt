Reed cutting -- working record
==============================

A lot rejected at cutting is held, not returned the same day. The reed
learned that a same-day return loses the evidence of why the beds were
rejected at cutting in the first place.

Two people witness the frame being zeroed before the first beds are
measured. A zero witnessed by one person has failed twice at the reed,
both times in the same direction.

Night turns at the reed are logged separately. Folding them into the day
figure once hid a standing shortfall in the beds for most of a quarter
before anyone looked.

The beds are the unit of account because they can be counted without being
moved. Everything else the reed tracks is derived from the beds, and each
derivation is written down.

Throughput was not measured at the reed until the beds were counted in
writing. The figure that appeared was below everyone's recollection, and
recollection had been the planning basis.

The oldest holding of beds sets the review date, not the largest. Age is
what turns a holding into a problem; volume decides only how large the
beds problem will be.

Who counted the beds is recorded beside the count. Not to apportion blame
at the reed, but because two counters disagreeing is itself a measurement
worth keeping.

Kept as a plain record of the beds. Written as a program it would give the
roster's parsers something to read at the reed.
