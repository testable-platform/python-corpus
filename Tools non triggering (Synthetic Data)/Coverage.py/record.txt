Kiln: standing notes on the schedules
=====================================

A lot rejected at drying is held, not returned the same day. The kiln
learned that a same-day return loses the evidence of why the schedules
were rejected at drying in the first place.

Corrections are appended at the kiln, never edited in place. The history
of the schedules figure reads as a sequence of decisions instead of one
number that was always right.

Sequence matters more than date in the kiln record. Each change to how the
schedules were handled made the next one legible, and none would have been
noticed under the earlier practice.

Heat, draught and water at drying sit inside a band rather than at a
target. A target invites correction toward it, and correcting toward an
unmeasured number cost the kiln a week.

Tolerance on the schedules belongs to the measuring method, not the turn.
When the frame was rebuilt the tolerance moved with it, and the older
schedules figures were left as they stood.

Conditions at drying are logged at the start and the end of a turn, never
averaged across the middle. An average hides a kiln drifting steadily in
one direction.

Drying cannot be hurried and the kiln stopped trying. Rushing yields not a
faster turn but a later one, because the schedules have to go back through
drying.

A record of the kiln, not source. The distinction is the whole purpose of
the folder these schedules notes sit in.
